Stone Library

Built fireproof in 1870 to hold the family’s books, exactly as John Quincy Adams asked in his will. Over 14,000 volumes in 14 languages, still on their original shelves. Part of the Peace field tour.

  • 20–30 min

Site

Dinner & the beach

Back to Marshfield around 6:00 PM. Dinner at the house and down to the water — or the Irish pub, if the day hasn’t worn everyone out.

  • Beach evening
  • Pub option

Two things to get right on this day. First, going inside any of the historic houses requires a guided tour, and those are reserved in person, same day, at the visitor center desk — which is why the 9:00 AM start matters. Second, the planning notes call this a Braintree day: the birthplaces did stand in Braintree, but its North Precinct became the town of Quincy in 1792, so everything is signposted as Quincy today. Visitor center and house tours generally run Wednesday to Sunday, 9:00–5:00, through the October season — Wednesday works, but it’s worth a call to confirm the week of.

Leyden Street & Cole’s Hill

Leyden Street is the first street the colonists laid out, in 1620. Cole’s Hill above it is where they buried their dead that first winter at night, so the Wampanoag wouldn’t see how few were left.
